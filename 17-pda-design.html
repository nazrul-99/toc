Designing pushdown automata

A PDA is a finite automaton with a stack: P = (Q, Σ, Γ, δ, q₀, Z₀, F). A move δ(q, a, X) = {(p, γ)} means: in state q, reading a (or ε, reading nothing), with X on top of the stack, go to p and replace X by the string γ (leftmost symbol on top). In this section acceptance is by final state: the input is accepted if, after reading all of it, the PDA can be in a state of F.

EffectLabel

The design procedure

  1. Decide what must be counted or matched. Push one symbol per unit to be counted.
  2. Use states for phases (reading a's, reading b's, …) and the stack for counts.
  3. Pop one symbol for every matching unit later in the input. A mismatch should simply have no move.
  4. Detect the end of matching by seeing Z₀ on top, then make an ε-move to a final state.
  5. Regular parts of a language (no counting) need only states; the stack can stay at Z₀.
  6. Check the boundary cases: ε, the shortest strings, and strings with one symbol too many.
