How to design an NFA

An NFA is like a DFA with two freedoms. From one state there may be several arrows on the same symbol, and there may be no arrow at all. The machine accepts a string if at least one sequence of choices ends in a final state. A branch that meets a missing arrow simply dies.

PowerEqual. Every NFA can be converted to a DFA by the subset construction (Section 5), though the DFA may need up to 2ⁿ states.

The guess-and-verify procedure

  1. Write short examples in and out of L, including ε.
  2. Find the "where" question. Many languages ask about a pattern at an unknown position (ends with, contains, k-th from the right). Let the NFA guess that position.
  3. Skip with a self loop. A start state that loops on every symbol reads the part before the guess.
  4. Verify with a chain. After the guess, check the pattern deterministically with one state per symbol.
  5. Let wrong guesses die. Leave out every arrow that would break the pattern. No trap and no failure arrows are needed.
  6. Decide whether the final state loops. "Contains": the final state absorbs everything. "Ends with" or "k-th from the right": the final state has no outgoing arrows.
  7. Union is free. For "A or B", send one branch for each from the start state.
  8. Test, especially the shortest strings and the strings just outside L.
